01 Context and problem
In early 2020, the Ministry of Economy was piloting a local marketplace: a platform to connect people with neighborhood businesses within a 3 km radius, through Gloria, a virtual assistant.
When the pandemic hit, the scope changed overnight. The pilot became a national program. The challenge was no longer just enabling local discovery — it was the economic survival of thousands of small businesses with no digital presence.
The problem
SMBs without digital channels
Only 13% did e-commerce. Only 46% had websites. Lockdown closed physical commerce instantly.
The opportunity
Conversational AI as a bridge
An assistant that could find and recommend nearby businesses, available 24/7, without requiring an app.
02 Defining Gloria's personality
The first thing I ruled out was defining Gloria's personality alone. I facilitated a co-creation workshop with Ministry stakeholders, SMB owners from Barrio Italia, and program coordinators. The goal was to build a character that could genuinely represent thousands of different businesses without sounding generic or corporate.
"The workshop showed that both groups wanted Gloria to sound like a well-informed neighbor, not like a customer service bot."
Four communication pillars emerged from that workshop:
1
Accessible and warm
Language that works for all levels of digital literacy — no jargon or corporate tone.
2
Rooted in the local
Always speaks like someone who knows the neighborhood, not a centralized service.
3
Decisively helpful
Never leaves users in a dead end — always offers a clear next step.
4
Transparent about limitations
Honest when she doesn't have an answer, without breaking user trust.

03 Conversational flows
With personality defined, I mapped all possible conversation paths. The big challenge in conversational design is anticipating intent variation: people ask the same thing in dozens of different ways, and a chatbot that doesn't recognize this creates immediate frustration.
I organized the architecture around three primary intents — product search, location-based discovery, and contact/logistics information — with explicit fallback routes and graceful failure states.

04 The COVID pivot
Halfway through the project, scope shifted from one Santiago district to the entire country. Key decisions I made in response:
1
Optional geolocation
Rural users needed to navigate by district or region, not just proximity.
2
Minimal onboarding for SMBs
Registration form reduced to the absolute minimum — completable on mobile, no technical knowledge required.
3
Explicit empty states
Clear messaging for districts without registered SMBs yet, to maintain user trust.

06 Learnings
Personality is not a deliverable — it's a research output
The most common mistake in conversational AI design is treating personality as a creative brief. Gloria's identity emerged from what stakeholders actually valued. That co-creation step is what made her feel authentic.
Designing for failure matters more than designing for success
The moments when the AI doesn't understand or has no answer are what define the entire experience. I spent as much time on fallback flows as on the main responses.
An AI product has two groups of users
SMBs weren't just end users — they were the data source that made Gloria useful. Designing SMB onboarding with the same care as buyer experience is what let the supply side scale quickly.
